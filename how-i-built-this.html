How I built this site

This whole page is a bit long because I believe design is for everyone! Especially with the tools we have access to today, I think that anyone is capable of building cool and creative things — it's just a matter of knowing how to use everything.

Below, I'm walking through the whole process: how I actually think and work (scrapped ideas, notes-app chaos, and all), where AI fit in — and very much didn't — how I planned and researched before touching Figma, the interaction decisions I made along the way, and the hand-drawn assets and animations that ended up being the most time-consuming (BUT most fun) part of the whole thing.

how I work / how my mind operates
  • Over the summer, I would walk to the gym A LOT, which meant I had some time to just be with myself and my thoughts — I quickly realized that a lot of my best ideas came up during this time (check out my Strava to see it documented…lol).
  • Whenever an idea came to me, I wrote it down as fast as possible. If I was on the go, I would jot it down in my notes app, or if I was on my laptop, I had Claude + Notion MCP make me task organization where I could plug in tasks, and I would check them off.
  • From there, I give myself deadlines so the idea can actually be executed. For this portfolio, I knew I wanted to get it out before September 1st (before school got really busy and before most major companies drop their summer internship roles), so being organized and consistent was a top priority for me.
working WITH ai, instead of ai working for me

To me, a portfolio shouldn't just be a vehicle to display a designer's work. Instead, it should showcase who the designer is at their core (and also, of course, display their work). That's why I only used AI with more menial tasks related to optimizing my workflow — like organizing tasks/feedback, tracking progress, setting deadlines, replicating pages, basic layouts, compressing or cropping images, and coding — instead of it fully doing the work for me and producing AI slop. Because of this, I made sure the portfolio truly reflected my own thinking.

planning stages

One of my FAVORITE quotes that constantly drives my curiosity and passion for design is from Austin Kleon's book, Steal Like An Artist: "If you steal from one author, it's plagiarism; if you steal from many, it's research."

So before even starting to design my portfolio, I stalked so many portfolios and combined them in a document where I noted down specific interactions (take a look here!) — heads up, I ended up taking my actual notes out, so it's really just a list of the portfolios I looked at with links to each. I found that the most helpful portfolios were the ones that detailed their process and thought process behind their designs — so I hope to do the same!

early (and scrapped) ideas

After talking with Bruce Cao, he asked me a lot of thought-provoking questions like, "What are the fish supposed to tell me about YOU?" which pivoted the way I viewed portfolios as a whole and led me to creating something that truly felt like "me."

balancing fun and practicality through interactions

I wanted to fill the site with fun, playful interactions, but I also wanted them to be noticeable enough that people would actually stop and engage with them — without going so far that it felt overstimulating.

This is probably where I poured the most time into the whole project — every little doodle and drawing here is hand-drawn.

The animations are hand-drawn too, frame by frame. I had zero prior animation experience, so I leaned on Pinterest references to figure it out. Once I got the hang of it, though, I had so much fun coming up with new illustrations to add (this one is my current favorite).

what I learned
  • If you have an idea, act on it ASAP. Because either you're going to forget after a few minutes or someone else is going to think of it. Some actually, most of my best ideas came to me very randomly, but I'm SO glad I wrote them down and executed them.
  • Being inspired is important, but it’s also equally important to not get bogged down in aimlessly looking for inspo (because then you’re just wasting time…)
  • Being organized is SUCH a big deal and sped up the process SOOO much when I figured out an efficient way to work. I could literally just plug and chug any ideas I wanted to implement — this was especially helpful when I got feedback from others.
  • Obviously, when you give Claude or Cursor a prompt, it's going to take some time to execute it, so during that downtime I made sure I was either working on an illustration/animation, writing up a case study, finding design inspo — just doing anything to add to my portfolio instead of sitting idly or doomscrolling.
